# Milestone 3 Review — Open-World Sandbox

> QA Engineer and Critical Reviewer · 2026-11-23

## Delivered (5 commits)

1. **Street generation v2** (organic grid): seeded block sizes; superblocks from removed road segments, chosen as a matching in the block-adjacency graph, which provably keeps the road network connected; T-junctions and L-bends; deep superblocks with inner building rings and garden courts; a fountain square; crosswalks only between sidewalks, never under parked cars.
2. **Vehicles and traffic**:
   - a road network model (right-hand lanes, junction exits, curved turn paths, timed signals at 3- and 4-way junctions);
   - four vehicle classes; parked cars are real, stealable vehicles;
   - AI traffic that follows lanes, turns, stops at red lights and for pedestrians and Wildlings, and honks;
   - player driving with arcade physics and a handbrake drift, plus collisions, damage, burning wrecks and explosions;
   - carjacking; speed-dependent camera zoom with look-ahead; headlights, brake lights and smoke.
3. **Wardens**:
   - witness model: police see crimes directly; civilians phone them in after a delay; Saints never talk;
   - heat turns into 1–5 wanted stars and decays once you're out of sight;
   - one ambient patrol cruiser, plus pursuit cruisers that route greedily and then chase and ram;
   - officers on foot and arrests; getting boxed in while driving; busted (fine and release at the substation); resprays at Rustbucket Garage.
4. **Factions and economy**:
   - reputation tiers for 5 factions plus the Wardens, driven by actions and mission rewards;
   - per-block influence with an hourly Saints-vs-Halcyon contest that the player's standing tilts;
   - state dressing in the world (Halcyon posters, Saints halos), and HUD turf;
   - prices with a daily swing plus territory and standing modifiers;
   - a black market for Wildlings with dynamic demand;
   - standing-aware Saints dialogue and clinic fees;
   - pause-menu Map (territory, landmarks, objective markers) and Factions tabs.
5. **Procedural synthwave soundtrack v1**:
   - a seeded, rule-based composer (handwritten progression, arpeggio and drum tables, plus the main-theme leitmotif);
   - a Web Audio synth rack (pads, bass, arp, lead, drums, reverb, delay, ducking);
   - a bar-quantised mood director (title, Slums night/day, battle, chase), a volume option, and tab-hidden suspension.

## Critical review

- *Vehicle physics in the simulation, not Phaser Arcade/Matter* (ADR-015). Keeps determinism, save validation and headless tests. The cost is simplified collision (two-circle bodies and sampled OBB vs tiles), which is adequate for a top-down game.
- *Greedy police routing.* Cheap and good enough on a connected grid; it can loop around superblocks. Hierarchical A* on the road graph is planned with the city graph in M4.
- *Faction strategy is a hand-tuned rule pair, not GOAP yet.* Deliberate: the data model (influence per block, reputation, events) is what M4's planner will drive. The rules already meet "two factions operate independently and change territory".
- *Music in the browser is untestable by ear in CI.* We test the composer (pure) and the engine's wiring; mixing is a human task for M7.
- **Rejected:** non-integer camera zoom while driving. Kept, because the smoothness outweighs slight shimmer; revisit with real art.
